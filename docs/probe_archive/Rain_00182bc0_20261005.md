## Recovered behavior

The task allocates its rain-drop pool, motion histories, timers, particle
atomic, frame, and output matrix in its initialization state. Its update
state spawns available drops, applies camera vectors and motion histories,
writes matrix and color streams, retires expired drops, and queues the
resulting atomic. Disabled tasks and unknown states return without updates.

The recovery uses complete objects with the layouts consumed by their real
providers: twelve-byte vectors, sixty-four-byte matrices, sixteen-byte drop
records, and eight-byte PTank lock records. Matrix assignments include their
flags and SDK padding words. Lock records keep data and byte stride together;
matrix and color output advance by their respective provider-supplied strides.

Allocation and loop values have distinct semantic lifetimes. The allocation
dispatch slot stays live across allocation callbacks, while its function
pointer is read again at each call. Pool and output-matrix pointers are
reloaded after callbacks that may replace them. Camera vectors are copied
and normalized as complete vectors before producing each trail matrix.

The trail-render loop and the expired-drop reset loop deliberately share
their counter. The reset loop advances that counter as in retail; replacing
it with an unrelated counter changes subsequent loop behavior. The compiler's
loop-invariant setting is confined to this function with pragma push/pop.
No assembly computation, dummy local, forced register, or invented padding
is used to reproduce the instruction sequence.
