## Complete objects and actual contracts

Both ports use an array of two complete 28-byte camera poses. Each pose owns
its three-float position and four-float quaternion. `func_001bd560` copies
all 28 bytes, and `func_001bac20` consumes all 28 bytes from each pose while
constructing four interpolation keys. The target calls pass the complete
pose addresses, not an undersized position buffer.

The quaternion views match the named, complete 16-byte types in the actual
`func_001ec2b0` provider. Its declarations and call-site casts within both
owners now agree. The blend cache owns two quaternions, a float scalar, and
an integer flag: 40 bytes, matching the assembly provider's stores through
offset `0x24`.

The second P3 donor used explicit padding members in `B41Work`. None is
carried into P4. Separate candidate, direction, center, near-point,
horizontal vector, quaternion, cache, and pose locals naturally produce the
retail stack layout. No assembly, artificial arguments, padding stores, or
optimization directives are introduced.

The P4 quaternion transformer returns its destination pointer. The camera
owner now declares that return and its real vector/quaternion parameters;
five existing call sites receive explicit pointer-view casts. The existing
unit-position declaration is corrected to `BtlUnit*` and `RwV3d*`, with
three existing integer-address call sites cast explicitly. These changes
preserve the complete owner object outside the recovered function.

The first port also uses the quaternion rotation provider's actual pointer
return, float angle argument, and integer combine mode. The tangent and
normalization providers retain their float argument/return contracts. The
two polynomial leading-coefficient references remain distinct P4 globals,
`fGpffff8180` and `fGpffff8194`, even though their retail values agree.

Seven active C providers were independently compiled in six complete
configured owners and compared to retail: `001bd560`, `00194ff0`,
`001958f0`, `001ec2b0`, `001bac20`, `001bbef0`, and `003e4180`. All are exact.
Their frozen source, objects, resolved bytes, and receipts are retained.
Assembly-only quaternion/cache/normalization provider contracts were checked
against their P4 retail implementations.

The retail references to the recovered functions occur at `0x005f76a0`
and `0x005f7524`: field zero of entries 24 and 5 in `D_005F74C0`.

This establishes the recovered callbacks' argument and return contracts.

## Native behavior checks

GCC and Clang each pass **8,192 scenarios and 2,806,418 checks** on native
i386 with undefined-behavior traps enabled. Eight unchanged function bodies
are executed: both recoveries plus the actual pose copy, unit sphere-position,
quaternion-angle, frame-consumer, duration, and vector-length bodies.

The fixture checks both sides and equality at the angle thresholds, both
cache modes, signed quaternion representations, several field-of-view,
radius, scale, height, and distance values, the 25-unit height clamp, and the
600-unit distance minimum. An independent expected camera image checks all
four complete pose keys, timing and index fields, preserved current-pose
bytes, and surrounding guards. It also checks call arguments and phase counts.

Lens, inverse-cosine, look-at, vector-transform, normalization, cache setup,
and interpolation backends are controlled test boundaries. These tests do
not emulate PS2 floating-point behavior or certify arbitrary nonfinite
geometry. The exact retail byte proof supplies the target instruction
equivalence. The unchanged consumer's constant-true local-array address
guards produce four GCC and two Clang warnings; those warnings are retained
and nonfatal for that consumer alone. Both proposed production owners are
lint-clean, with zero findings.
